# BTEC Online Website Super Prompt

## Context

You are building the website and marketing copy for an AI-native online BTEC Business qualification company. The founder, Rhys, has direct BTEC teaching experience and is launching with a phased market validation approach. This prompt guides website copy, form strategy, ad messaging, and international targeting before building compliance infrastructure or the full operational system.

## Business Model (Quick Reminder)

**The offer**: Affordable online BTEC Business qualifications with qualified teacher support, structured assignments, AI-assisted learning resources, and progress tracking. Lower cost than private schools, but serious credentials and real teaching.

**Positioning line**: "Recognised vocational qualifications, qualified teachers, online delivery, and AI-supported learning at a fraction of private-school cost."

**Core promise**: "95% of previous students achieved distinctions. Graduate with three distinctions in BTEC Business and open doors to universities and beyond."

**Target market (test both, expect international first)**: Expat families, internationally mobile families, homeschool families, and families in British-influenced cities choosing between expensive international schools and affordable online pathways.

**Key geographies to test (Phase 1)**: UK, Dubai, Singapore, Hong Kong, Nairobi, Kuala Lumpur. Compare cost per lead, booked calls, price sensitivity, and market strength.

---

## Phase 1: Market Validation (This Phase)

### Goal
Prove that families will book a call, engage with your pitch, and indicate willingness to pay before investing in compliance, full curriculum, or systems build.

### What Counts as Validation
- Landing page traffic
- Form submissions (leads)
- Booked consultation calls
- Price sensitivity signals
- Geographic demand mapping
- Competitive positioning clarity (local schools vs. international schools vs. private tutors)

### What Does NOT Happen in Phase 1
- Official Pearson centre approval (comes in Phase 2)
- Full curriculum development (comes in Phase 2)
- Student enrolment into official qualifications (comes in Phase 2)
- Centre partnership agreements (comes in Phase 2)
- Compliance infrastructure (comes in Phase 2)

   - Homeschooled

4. **"Which of these matters most to you?"** (Select up to 2)

   - Online flexibility
   - Recognised qualification

5. **"Where are you located?"**
   - UK
   - UAE / Middle East
   - Singapore / Southeast Asia
   - Hong Kong / East Asia
   - East Africa
   - Other

6. **"What's your approximate budget for a year of study?"**

## Pricing Test Strategy

### Do NOT lock in one price. Test three price points via ads.

**Founding Cohort Offer (Price Test 1)**: £2,000/year
- Positioned as: "Early supporter rate. Founding cohort gets priority support and input on course structure."
- Payment: Annual (full) or £180/month (12 months)

**Standard Offer (Price Test 2)**: £3,500/year
- Positioned as: "Full support: live lessons, feedback, 1-to-1 sessions, progress reports."
- Payment: Annual or £295/month

**Premium Offer (Price Test 3)**: £4,800/year
- Positioned as: "Intensive pathway. 2 weekly 1-to-1s, personalised learning plan, priority access."
- Payment: Annual or £400/month

### How to Test
- Run identical ads with different landing page URLs or form fields
- Track which price point generates the most leads
- Compare cost per lead by price and geography
- Don't assume lower price wins—premium positioning often converts better with the right audience

## Sample Lessons Strategy (Phase 1)

You do NOT need the full curriculum yet. But you need proof-of-concept materials.

### Minimum Viable Sample (Week 1 of Unit 1: "Business Environment")

1. **One recorded lesson** (8-10 mins)
   - Topic: "What is a business? Why does it matter?"
   - Format: You on camera, simple whiteboard, one key diagram
   - Tone: Clear, encouraging, not cheesy

2. **One assignment brief** (1 page)
   - Task: Analyse a simple real business (e.g., a local school, a coffee shop)
   - Evidence needed: 300-400 word report
   - Assessment criteria: Clarity, accuracy, application

3. **One feedback template** (showing what good feedback looks like)
   - Example: "You correctly identified three business functions. Next step: explain *why* businesses need each one. See the criteria checklist."

4. **One progress checklist** (what learners need to complete this unit)
   - Lesson 1: Watch & summarise
   - Assignment 1: Submit report
   - Feedback: Receive & respond
   - Lesson 2: Watch & build on feedback
   - (Etc.)

**Why this matters**: When someone books a call, you can say, "Here's exactly how we teach. Here's exactly what we ask learners to do. Here's the feedback you'll get." It's not the full offer, but it's proof.

## Consultation Call Script (Phase 1)

This is a **listening call**, not a sales call. You're learning.

### Call Structure (15 mins)

1. **Warm-up (2 mins)**
   - "Thanks for booking. Tell me a bit about your family and why you're exploring BTEC Online."

2. **Understand the context (4 mins)**
   - "Are you currently in school, homeschooled, or exploring alternatives?"
   - "What's drawing you to BTEC specifically?"
   - "What concerns do you have about online learning?"

3. **Share your offer (3 mins)**
   - Walk through: Live lessons, recorded library, assignment support, feedback, progress tracking
   - Show the sample lesson (share screen)
   - Mention: "We're in market validation phase right now. You'd be an early supporter."

4. **Price conversation (3 mins)**
   - "For a year of BTEC Business with full support, we're testing pricing around £2,000–£3,500/year. Does that feel realistic to you?"
   - Listen for: Sticker shock? Relief? Questions?
   - **Don't promise anything yet.** "We're finalising our pathway and would love your input."

5. **Next steps (3 mins)**
   - "Would you want to join a pilot when we launch?" (Add to waitlist)
   - "Could I send you more details in a week or so?" (Email follow-up)
   - "Any questions I haven't answered?"

### What You're Learning
- Pain point clarity: Is it cost? Flexibility? School refusal? University progression?
- Price sensitivity: Do they flinch at £2k or ask about £5k?
- Timeline: Do they need to start in September or flexible?
- Competitive positioning: Are they comparing to local schools, international schools, tutors, or homeschool?
- Market signal: Is this person serious or just curious?

## Feedback Loop (Phase 1 Learnings to Capture)

After every 10 calls, update your understanding of:

1. **Cost sensitivity by geography**
   - UK families: What's the resistance price? What's the sweet spot?
   - Dubai families: Different affordability?
   - Singapore: Different again?

2. **Pain points**
   - Is it "school is too expensive"? (Cost-driven)
   - Is it "school doesn't fit my child's learning style"? (Quality/fit-driven)
   - Is it "we're moving a lot and need flexibility"? (Mobility-driven)
   - Is it "my child is gifted and bored"? (Acceleration-driven)

3. **Competitive landscape**
   - Are families comparing BTEC Online to local schools?
   - Or to expensive international schools?
   - Or to tutors + homeschool?
   - (This changes your positioning)

4. **Content gaps**
   - Do families ask about university recognition in their country?
   - Do they ask about peer interaction / community?
   - Do they ask about careers support after BTEC?

5. **Timeline**
   - Do most want to start September?
   - Or are multiple intakes (January, April) actually valuable?

6. **Subject expansion**
   - Do you get BTEC Business questions only?
   - Or do families ask about BTEC IT, Health, Science?

## Phase 1 Success Metrics

You're not looking for massive scale. You're looking for proof.

### Targets (First Month or Two)

- **Website traffic**: 500-1,000 unique visitors
- **Form submissions**: 30-50 leads
- **Booked calls**: 10-15
- **Call show-up rate**: 60%+
- **Families interested in pilot**: 5-10

### What "Proof" Looks Like

✅ At least 3 families in two different geographies seriously considering it
✅ Consistent price-point feedback (e.g., "£2,500 feels right")
✅ Clear pain point pattern (e.g., "Parents are paying £20k/year for school, want alternatives")
✅ Geographic demand signal (e.g., "Dubai is stronger than UK")
✅ Proof that people will book a call (demand exists)

### If You DON'T Get Proof

- Pivot messaging (maybe it's not price, it's flexibility)
- Pivot geography (maybe UK is harder, international stronger)
- Pivot target (maybe it's not families, it's tutors reselling to families)
- Kill the idea before you build the whole thing

## Phase 2 Triggers (What Unlocks the Build)

You move to Phase 2 (compliance, curriculum, systems) **only when**:

1. You've had 10+ serious conversations showing demand
2. At least 5 families have indicated "yes, I'd pilot this"
3. Price-point data shows clear willingness to pay
4. Geographic data shows which markets to prioritize
5. You've validated that Pearson approval / centre partnership is feasible
6. You're confident enough to build the full offer

## Key Messaging Rules for Phase 1

### DO Say
- "We're validating demand for this offer"
- "We're building this based on what families actually need"
- "95% of my previous students achieved distinctions"
- "This is a serious qualification from a serious teacher"
- "We're pursuing Pearson centre approval as part of our build"
- "Pricing is flexible for founding cohorts"
- "We're testing multiple geographies and intakes"

### DON'T Say
- "We're officially accredited" (not yet)
- "Guaranteed university admission" (not your place)
- "Guaranteed grades" (not ethical)
- "AI writes your assignments" (it doesn't)
- "This is the only way to succeed" (it's not)
- "We're the cheapest option" (you're not, and that's not the pitch)

## One More Thing: Set a Phase 1 Timeline

**Do not let Phase 1 drag on.** Give yourself 4-6 weeks to get enough data to decide.

**Suggested Timeline**:
- **Week 1-2**: Launch website + ads + sample lessons
- **Week 3-4**: Get 20-30 leads, book 5-10 calls
- **Week 5-6**: Complete calls, gather data, analyse patterns
- **Week 7**: Decision: Pivot, kill, or move to Phase 2

If after 6 weeks you haven't booked 10 serious calls, something needs to change (messaging, price, geography, targeting). Don't keep running ads at the same pace hoping it'll work eventually.
