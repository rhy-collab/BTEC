## What This Is

A 6-week market validation phase that tests whether families will pay for an AI-native, online BTEC Business school. We test across 6 international geographies using identical messaging, collect leads through a validation-form landing page, and book consultation calls to learn what families actually want.

**If Phase 1 validates → Phase 2 (Pearson approval, full curriculum, operations)**  
**If Phase 1 doesn't validate → Pivot or kill**

---

## Why Market Validation First

### The Risk We're Avoiding
Building an academy for a market that doesn't exist. The sunk cost fallacy is real: once you invest in curriculum, compliance, and systems, it's psychologically hard to stop — even when the market isn't there.

### What We Get Instead
- **Demand proof**: Do families actually want this? Or just "that sounds nice"?
- **Price sensitivity**: What are they actually willing to pay?
- **Geographic winner**: Which market is 2–3× stronger than others?
- **Pain point clarity**: What's the real problem — cost, flexibility, school refusal, university progression?
- **Messaging validation**: Does our core value prop resonate, or does it need refining?

### The Cost
**£500–£2,000** on ads + calls. Worth thousands in avoided wrong bets.

## The Six Test Markets

### 1. 🇦🇪 Dubai, UAE
- **Why**: Largest expat hub in the Middle East. Private school fees £15k–25k/year. Families actively seek alternatives.
- **Hypothesis**: Strongest conversion. High price tolerance. Willing to pay £3k–5k.
- **Tweak**: Currency in AED. Proof point: "Trusted by expat families in Dubai."

### 2. 🇸🇬 Singapore
- **Why**: Major expat hub. Strong British school influence. Highly competitive education market.
- **Hypothesis**: Strong conversion. Moderate price sensitivity.
- **Tweak**: Currency in SGD. Proof point: "Recognised by global universities."

### 3. 🇭🇰 Hong Kong
- **Why**: Intensive school-choice culture. High willingness to pay for credible qualifications.
- **Hypothesis**: Strong conversion. Low price sensitivity.
- **Tweak**: Currency in HKD. Proof point: "British qualification, globally recognised."

### 4. 🇲🇾 Kuala Lumpur
- **Why**: Growing expat community. British-influenced schools. Underexplored market.
- **Hypothesis**: Moderate conversion. Price-sensitive but responsive.
- **Tweak**: Currency in MYR.

### 5. 🇰🇪 Nairobi, Kenya
- **Why**: Strong British educational tradition. Emerging middle class. Underserved market for online British qualifications.
- **Hypothesis**: Moderate conversion. Price-sensitive but serious intent. May surprise.
- **Tweak**: Currency in KES. Proof point: "British qualification pathway."

### 6. 🇬🇧 UK
- **Why**: Baseline. Direct competition from local schools, colleges, and other online providers.
- **Hypothesis**: Lowest conversion. Highest competition. Families can access free or subsidised alternatives.
- **Tweak**: Currency in GBP.

## Landing Page Strategy

### 95% Identical Across Markets
Same headline, same features, same pricing, same form. Only currency and one proof point change.

**Why?** Tests whether the core value prop is universally strong. If it works in multiple geographies, you have a global product. If it only works in one, you learn the market or message needs refinement.

### Core Value Prop (Everywhere)
- **Headline**: "An Affordable Online Route Through BTEC Business"
- **Proof**: "95% of previous students achieved distinctions"
- **Features**: Teachers, structure, cost, qualifications, flexibility, AI support
- **Pricing**: £2k (founding), £3.5k (standard), £4.8k (premium)

### Landing Page Sections
1. Hero (headline + CTA: "Book a Free Consultation")
2. Problem (private school too expensive, traditional education not flexible enough)
3. Solution (AI-native, teacher-led, Pearson-aligned BTEC)
4. Features (qualified teachers, structured curriculum, AI support, flexible schedule, globally recognised qualification)
5. Pricing (3 tiers, annual/monthly)
6. Form (7 validation questions)
7. Testimonials (placeholder for Phase 1 learnings)
8. FAQ (accreditation, assessment, language, time commitment, technology)
9. Footer

## Form Strategy

### Not Just Email Capture
Every question provides validation data. The form is a research instrument disguised as a signup form.

### Questions
1. **How did you hear about us?** — Source tracking (Google, Facebook, referral, other)
2. **Which qualification are you interested in?** — Business only or broader interest?
3. **What's your current situation?** — In school, homeschooled, left school, working, expat family?
4. **What matters most to you?** — Cost, flexibility, qualification recognition, teacher quality, multiple intakes?
5. **Where are you located?** — Geographic validation
6. **What's your budget range?** — Under £2k / £2k–3.5k / £3.5k–5k / £5k+
7. **Preferred next step?** — Book a call / Send more info / See sample lesson

### What Each Question Tells You
- **Source**: Which channel and geography drive the most engaged leads
- **Qualification**: Whether to expand beyond Business or stay focused
- **Situation**: Whether you're attracting school refusers, homeschoolers, expats, or career changers
- **Pain point**: What message to emphasise in Phase 2
- **Location**: Which markets are actually responding
- **Budget**: Where to set final pricing
- **Next step**: How much friction leads can tolerate

## Pricing Test Strategy

### Why Three Tiers
- Tests the full range of price sensitivity
- Premium tier makes Standard look reasonable (anchoring effect)
- Founding tier creates urgency and exclusivity
- Data shows which price converts best in which geography

### Test Method
Create three landing page variants (or use UTM params + dynamic pricing display) and track conversion by geography and channel.

### Key Question
**Does lower price = higher conversion?**  
Not necessarily. Premium positioning often converts better with expat/international families who equate price with quality.

## Sample Materials (Build Before Launch)

### 1. Recorded Lesson (8–10 mins)
- **Topic**: "What is a Business? Why Does It Matter?" (Unit 1, Business Environment)
- **Format**: You on camera, whiteboard, one key diagram
- **Upload**: YouTube (unlisted) or Vimeo
- **Link from**: Landing page hero CTA and email 1

### 2. Assignment Brief (1 page)
- **Task**: "Analyse a business you know"
- **Output**: 300–400 word report
- **Criteria**: Clarity, accuracy, application of concepts
- **Purpose**: Shows exactly what learners do

### 3. Feedback Template
- **Sample feedback**: "You correctly identified three functions. Next step: explain *why* each function matters. See the criteria checklist attached."
- **Purpose**: Shows quality of feedback and support

### 4. Unit Checklist
- Watch lesson → Take notes
- Summarise key concepts
- Submit assignment
- Receive feedback
- Respond to feedback
- Revision session
- Assessment prep
- Final assessment

## Consultation Call Script

### Structure
**Duration**: 15 minutes  
**Tone**: Listening, learning, validating

### 1. Opening (2 min)
- "Thanks for booking this call. I'm excited to learn about your family."
- "This is a no-pressure conversation. We're building something new and want to make sure it serves families like yours."

### 2. Discovery (8 min)
- "Tell me about your family. Why are you exploring BTEC Online?"
- "What's your current situation? In school, homeschooled, exploring alternatives?"
- "What's drawing you to BTEC specifically?"
- "What concerns do you have about online learning?"
- "What's your biggest worry about your child's education right now?"

### 3. Show & Test (3 min)
- Screen share sample lesson
- "This is what a typical lesson looks like"
- "We're testing pricing around £2–3.5k/year. Does that feel realistic for your family?"
- "We're in market validation, so you'd be an early supporter with input on the course."

### 4. Next Steps & Close (2 min)
- "What would need to be true for you to commit?"
- "On a scale of 1–5, how likely are you to pilot this?"
- "I'll send you the sample lesson and some info. Happy to chat again."

### What to Log After Each Call
- Pain point (cost, flexibility, school fit, university progression, something else)
- Price tolerance (flinch at £2k, comfortable at £3.5k, ask about £5k)
- Timeline (must start September, flexible, exploring)
- Competition (comparing to what?)
- Likelihood to pilot (1–5)
- Key quotes (verbatim, for social proof later)

## Messaging Rules

### DO Say
- "We're validating demand for this offer"
- "95% of my previous students achieved distinctions"
- "We're pursuing Pearson centre approval"
- "AI-assisted, not AI-written"
- "Qualified teachers provide feedback"
- "You'd be an early supporter with input on the course"

### DON'T Say
- "We're officially accredited" (not yet)
- "Guaranteed university admission"
- "Guaranteed grades"
- "AI writes your work"
- "We're a school" (you're an online programme)
- "We have thousands of students" (you don't yet)

## Success Metrics

### Hard Targets (6 Weeks)
- 500–1,000 website visitors
- 30–50 form submissions
- 10–15 booked calls
- 60%+ call show-up rate
- 5–10 families indicating "I'd pilot this"

### What Counts as Proof
- 3+ families in 2+ geographies seriously interested
- Consistent price feedback across calls
- Clear pain point pattern
- Geographic winner identified (2–3× stronger than others)
- Willingness to book calls (demand exists)

### If You Don't Hit These
- Pivot messaging (maybe it's flexibility, not cost)
- Pivot geography (international, not UK)
- Pivot target (tutors, not families)
- **Kill before investing further**
