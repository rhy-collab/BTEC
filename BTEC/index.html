BTEC explained for parents

What exactly is this qualification?

It is a UK-designed Pearson BTEC International Level 3 Extended Diploma in Business. Pearson describes it as a two-year, full-time programme equivalent in size to three International A Levels. Instead of placing most of the pressure on final examinations, learners demonstrate business knowledge across a series of applied units.

How is it different from A Levels?

A Levels usually depend heavily on subject examinations. This BTEC route is applied and assignment-led: learners investigate businesses, analyse evidence, plan campaigns and events, make decisions, write reports and sometimes present their findings. Three units use Pearson Set Assignments and some work may be completed under controlled conditions, so “no traditional final exams” is more accurate than promising “no assessment pressure”.

How assessment works

Progress is built unit by unit.

The Extended Diploma has six mandatory units. Three are internally assessed and three use Pearson Set Assignments. At least nine additional optional units complete the full 15-unit programme.

Download the Pearson specification ↓
01

Internal assignments

Centre-set work can include investigations, reports, plans, presentations and practical evidence. The exact evidence is set by each authorised assignment brief.

3 mandatory units + selected optional units
02

Pearson Set Assignments

Pearson supplies the brief for Units 2, 6 and 7. Some tasks may require controlled conditions and must follow the published rules.

3 mandatory units
03

Pass, Merit and Distinction

Learners meet increasingly demanding criteria. Strong work moves beyond description into analysis, justified decisions and evaluation.

Criteria-led grading
04

Teaching and permitted feedback

Rhys teaches the knowledge, models strong approaches and checks progress. Formal assessment feedback, resubmissions and retakes must follow Pearson and approved-centre rules.

Support with integrity

Pearson does not set a universal “8,000 or 10,000 words per unit” rule in this specification. Workload and evidence format vary by unit and assignment brief. Families receive the exact unit plan and assessment calendar before enrolment.

Curriculum guide

The first ten Business units, in plain English.

These are Units 1–10 in Pearson’s specification. Units 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 are mandatory for the Extended Diploma; Units 5, 8, 9 and 10 are optional choices. A complete Extended Diploma requires 15 units, so five further optional units must also be agreed.

01

Exploring Business

Study business purpose, ownership, structure, stakeholders and the external environment.

Mandatory · Internal · 90 GLH
02

Research and Plan a Marketing Campaign

Use market research to understand customers and build a justified campaign plan.

Mandatory · Pearson Set Assignment · 90 GLH
03

Business Finance

Analyse financial information, assess business health and recommend improvements.

Mandatory · Internal · 90 GLH
04

Managing an Event

Work in a team to plan, coordinate and review a business or social-enterprise event.

Mandatory · Internal · 90 GLH
05

International Business

Explore global trade and the factors shaping international business strategies.

Optional · Internal · 60 GLH
06

Principles of Management

Understand how management and leadership contribute to workplace performance and success.

Mandatory · Pearson Set Assignment · 60 GLH
07

Business Decision Making

Bring ideas from across the course together to investigate evidence and justify a business decision.

Mandatory + synoptic · Pearson Set Assignment · 120 GLH
08

Human Resources

Learn how organisations plan, recruit, manage and support their people.

Optional · Internal · 60 GLH
09

Team Building in Business

Study team theory, group dynamics and practical ways to make teams effective.

Optional · Internal · 60 GLH
10

Recording Financial Transactions

Record transactions, check bank records and identify or correct financial errors.

Optional · Internal · 60 GLH

The year-by-year order is confirmed with the approved centre. We do not promise “five units in Year 1 and five in Year 2” because the official Extended Diploma requires 15 units and centres may sequence them differently.
