# BTEC Phase 1: Market Validation Strategy

## What Happened

You decided to phase the business into two stages: first proving demand through ads and calls, then building compliance and systems only if demand validates. This dramatically cuts risk and ensures you build for real customer needs, not assumptions. We created a comprehensive super-prompt that maps the entire Phase 1 strategy: website copy, form questions, pricing tests, ad strategy across six international markets, and what success looks like.

Phase 1 is about learning. Phase 2 (compliance, curriculum, operations) only happens if Phase 1 proves families will actually pay. This sequence protects you from building an entire academy for a market that doesn't exist.

## How We're Doing It

Phase 1 runs for 4-6 weeks and consists of five parallel workstreams: a clean landing page (already built), sample lessons (one recorded lesson + one assignment + feedback template), paid ads testing three price points across six markets (UK, Dubai, Singapore, Hong Kong, Nairobi, Kuala Lumpur), a form capturing validation signals (geography, pain point, budget sensitivity), and consultation calls where you listen more than sell.

You keep landing page copy 95% identical across all markets, only swapping currency and proof points. Ads and targeting change by geography. Everything feeds a central spreadsheet tracking: cost per lead, cost per booked call, show-up rates, price sensitivity by region, and stated pain points. The form asks not just for email, but "how did you hear about us," "what's your situation," "what matters most," and "what budget feels right."

## Why We're Doing It

Running full ads and collecting call data tells you within 6 weeks whether demand actually exists and where it's strongest. This costs a few hundred pounds and gives you data that's worth thousands in avoided investment in the wrong direction. You learn whether families will pay (£2k? £3.5k? £5k?), which geography is responsive (UK competitive, but Dubai or Singapore stronger?), and what the real pain points are (cost, flexibility, school refusal, university progression).

Only with this data do you move to Phase 2 (Pearson approval, full curriculum, learner management systems, compliance). If demand doesn't validate, you pivot the messaging or kill the idea. If it does, you build knowing exactly what to build and for whom.

## Test Markets & Geography Strategy

### Why These Six Cities

Each represents a different market dynamic:
- **UK**: Baseline. Local school competition, state schools, private schools all present. Tests positioning against direct competition.
- **Dubai**: Expat hub, high private-school fees (£15-25k+/year), families actively seeking alternatives. Strong willingness to pay signal.
- **Singapore**: Expat hub, British school influence (British schools very popular), high costs, price-sensitive segment exists.
- **Hong Kong**: Expat hub, British tradition, intensive school-choice culture, families actively comparing options.
- **Nairobi**: British school tradition, emerging middle class, price-sensitive but serious about qualifications, underserved market.
- **Kuala Lumpur**: British-influenced schools, expat families, growing middle class, underexplored market.

### How to Test

Run the exact same ads with identical creative and message. Track:
- **Cost per lead**: Which geography is cheapest to reach?
- **Booked call rate**: Which geography has highest intent?
- **Show-up rate**: Which geography actually shows up for calls?
- **Price sensitivity**: Where do families flinch at £2k vs. £3.5k?
- **Stated pain point**: Cost? Flexibility? School fit? University progression?

**Hypothesis**: International (Dubai, Singapore, Hong Kong) likely to convert faster than UK, because private-school fees are so much higher and families are actively seeking alternatives.

## Landing Page: 95% Same, Tiny Variations

### Consistent Across All Markets
- Headline: "An Affordable Online Route Through BTEC Business"
- Subheadline: Teacher-led, structured support, progress tracking, AI-assisted resources
- Proof point: "95% of previous students achieved distinctions. Graduate with three distinctions in BTEC Business."
- Features: Teachers, structure, cost, qualifications, flexibility, AI support
- Pricing: Show founding cohort (£2k), standard (£3.5k), premium (£4.8k)
- CTA: "Book a free consultation" or "Join the waitlist"

### Geographic Tweaks Only
- **Currency**: £ in UK, AED in Dubai, SGD in Singapore, HKD in Hong Kong, KES in Nairobi, MYR in Kuala Lumpur
- **Proof point tweak**:
  - UK: "British-qualified teacher, 8+ years BTEC classroom experience"
  - Dubai: "Trusted by expat families in Dubai and across the Gulf"
  - Singapore: "Recognised by UK and Singapore universities"
  - Hong Kong: "Alternative to expensive international schools"
  - Nairobi: "BTEC recognised across Commonwealth universities"

**Why 95%?** Testing whether your core value prop (price + teachers + recognised qualification) works universally. If demand is strong, the messaging works. If it's weak, you don't know if it's the message or the market—so keep it consistent.

## Form: Validation Questions (Not Just Email Capture)

### Question 1: "How did you hear about us?"
- Direct search / Google ad / Friend/family recommendation / Social media / Other
- **Learning**: Which channels work in which geographies?

### Question 2: "Which BTEC qualification?"
- BTEC Business Level 3 / Extended Diploma / Another subject / Not sure yet
- **Learning**: Demand for Business only, or should you expand faster?

### Question 3: "What's your current situation?"
- Currently in school / Homeschooled / Left school / Working / Expat/mobile
- **Learning**: Who's converting? Are you attracting families or lone learners?

### Question 4: "What matters most?" (Select 2)
- Lower cost / Online flexibility / Recognised qualification / Small classes / Multiple intakes/fast progression
- **Learning**: Cost-driven or quality-driven? Flexibility or credibility?

### Question 5: "Where are you located?"
- UK / UAE & Middle East / Singapore & Southeast Asia / Hong Kong & East Asia / East Africa / Other
- **Learning**: Direct geographic data. Compare CPL and conversion by region.

### Question 6: "Approximate annual budget?"
- Under £1,000 / £1-2k / £2-3.5k / £3.5-5k / £5k+
- **Learning**: Price ceiling by market. Are you positioned as budget or premium?

### Question 7: "Preferred next step?"
- Book consultation call / Send sample lessons first / Add to waitlist / Email me questions
- **Learning**: How much friction? Do people want to talk or need more proof first?

## Pricing Test Strategy

### Don't lock one price. Test three via ads.

**Founding Cohort**: £2,000/year (or local currency equivalent)
- Positioned as: "Early supporter rate. Founding cohort gets priority support and input on course structure."
- Payment: Annual or monthly installments (e.g., £180/mo)

**Standard**: £3,500/year
- Positioned as: "Full support—live lessons, feedback, 1-to-1 sessions, progress reports."
- Payment: Annual or £295/mo

**Premium**: £4,800/year
- Positioned as: "Intensive pathway—2 weekly 1-to-1s, personalised learning plan, priority access."
- Payment: Annual or £400/mo

### How to Test

Create three versions of your landing page (or use form fields) with different pricing. Run ads with UTM tags (e.g., `?price=founding`, `?price=standard`, `?price=premium`). Track:
- Which price point generates the most leads?
- Which converts to booked calls?
- Which has highest show-up rate?

**Don't assume lower price wins.** Premium positioning often converts better with the right audience (wealthy expat families). You're learning, not assuming.

## Sample Materials (Minimum Viable Proof)

You don't build the full curriculum yet. You build proof that your teaching method works.

### Deliverable 1: One Recorded Lesson (8-10 mins)
**Topic**: Unit 1, Business Environment — "What is a Business? Why Does It Matter?"  
**Format**: You on camera, simple whiteboard, one key diagram  
**Tone**: Clear, encouraging, real  
**Deliverable**: MP4 link, embeddable on landing page

### Deliverable 2: One Assignment Brief (1 page)
**Task**: Analyse a simple real business (local school, coffee shop, etc.)  
**Output**: 300-400 word report  
**Criteria**: Clarity, accuracy, application  
**Why**: Shows exactly what learners have to do

### Deliverable 3: One Feedback Template (Example)
**Sample feedback**: "You correctly identified three business functions. Next step: explain *why* businesses need each one. See the criteria checklist. Good work."  
**Why**: Shows quality of support learners will receive

### Deliverable 4: Unit Checklist (What's Involved)
- Lesson 1: Watch & summarise
- Assignment 1: Submit report
- Feedback: Receive & respond
- Lesson 2: Watch & build on feedback
- Revision session
- Assessment prep
- Final assessment
  
**Why**: Shows structure and commitment required

## Consultation Call Script (Phase 1 - Listening, Not Selling)

**Duration**: 15 minutes  
**Goal**: Understand pain point, learn price sensitivity, build relationship

### Call Structure

**Warm-up (2 min)**
- "Thanks for booking. Tell me a bit about your family and why you're exploring BTEC Online."

**Context (4 min)**
- "Are you currently in school, homeschooled, or exploring alternatives?"
- "What's drawing you to BTEC specifically?"
- "What concerns do you have about online learning?"

**Offer (3 min)**
- Walk through: Live lessons, recorded library, assignment support, feedback, progress tracking
- Show the sample lesson (screen share)
- Mention: "We're in market validation phase. You'd be an early supporter."

**Price Conversation (3 min)**
- "For a year of BTEC Business with full support, we're testing pricing around £2,000–£3,500/year. Does that feel realistic?"
- Listen for sticker shock, relief, or questions
- Don't promise anything: "We're finalising our pathway and value your input."

**Next Steps (3 min)**
- "Would you want to join a pilot when we launch?" (Waitlist)
- "Can I send you more details in a week or so?" (Email follow-up)
- "Any questions?"

### What You Learn
- Pain point: Cost? Flexibility? School refusal? University progression?
- Price sensitivity: Flinch at £2k or ask about £5k?
- Timeline: Start September or flexible?
- Competition: Comparing to local schools, international schools, tutors, or homeschool?
- Seriousness: Is this person genuinely interested or just curious?

### Post-Call Capture
After every call, log:
- Geography
- Pain point mentioned
- Budget comfort
- Timeline
- Comparison (what they're comparing you to)
- Likelihood to pilot (1-5)
- Follow-up action

## Success Metrics (Phase 1)

### Minimum Targets (First 4-6 Weeks)

- **Website traffic**: 500-1,000 unique visitors
- **Form submissions**: 30-50 leads
- **Booked calls**: 10-15
- **Call show-up rate**: 60%+
- **Families interested in pilot**: 5-10

### What Counts as "Proof"

✅ At least 3 families in two different geographies seriously considering it  
✅ Consistent price feedback (e.g., "£2.5k feels right")  
✅ Clear pain point pattern (e.g., parents paying £20k/year, seeking alternatives)  
✅ Geographic signal (e.g., Dubai stronger than UK)  
✅ Willingness to book calls (demand exists)  

### If You Don't Hit These

- Pivot messaging (maybe it's not about cost, it's about flexibility)
- Pivot geography (maybe UK is hard, international is stronger)
- Pivot target (maybe it's tutors, not families)
- Kill before investing further

## Messaging Rules (Phase 1)

### DO Say
- "We're validating demand for this offer"
- "We're building based on what families actually need"
- "95% of my previous students achieved distinctions"
- "This is a serious qualification from a serious teacher"
- "We're pursuing Pearson centre approval"
- "Pricing is flexible for founding cohorts"
- "We're testing multiple geographies and intakes"

### DON'T Say
- "We're officially accredited" (yet)
- "Guaranteed university admission"
- "Guaranteed grades"
- "AI writes your assignments"
- "We're the cheapest option"
- "This is the only way to succeed"

## Learning Capture (What to Track Weekly)

### After Every 5 Calls
- What pain point came up most?
- What price feedback did you hear?
- Which geography represented these calls?
- Any surprising objections or questions?

### After Every 10 Leads
- Compare geography performance (CPL, conversion rate, price sensitivity)
- Which channel generated best leads? (Search vs. social vs. display)
- Are you getting Business-specific interest or broad interest?
- Price point performance: Which attracted better leads?

### Weekly Ad Review
- Cost per lead by geography and price point
- Engagement rates (click-through, form submission)
- Cost trending (up or down)?

### Bi-Weekly Strategy Adjustment
- Should you increase spend in strongest geography?
- Should you shift messaging based on feedback?
- Should you pause a price point that's not converting?

## Phase 2 Triggers (When to Build)

You move to Phase 2 (Pearson approval, full curriculum, systems) **only when**:

1. ✅ 10+ serious conversations showing real demand
2. ✅ 5+ families indicating "yes, I'd pilot this"
3. ✅ Clear price-point data (families commit to specific price range)
4. ✅ Geographic winner (e.g., Dubai 3x stronger than UK)
5. ✅ Validated pain point (clear, repeating need)
6. ✅ Confirmed Pearson approval feasibility

## One Final Thing: Set a Deadline

**Do not let Phase 1 drag on.**

**Timeline**:
- Week 1-2: Launch everything
- Week 3-4: Collect leads, book calls
- Week 5-6: Complete calls, gather data
